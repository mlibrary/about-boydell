<h2>About Open Access and Opening the Future</h2>

<h3 id="section1.1">1. Why is Boydell &amp; Brewer offering this subscription membership model?</h3>

<p>We see participating in Opening the Future as a natural addition to our current engagement with other collective funding routes, and to our OA author fund - allowing us to fund more titles in a selection of our lists. Opening the Future enables us to continue diversifying and strengthening the paths available to our authors to publish their work open access.</p>

<p><a href="#top"><span style="font-size: 12px;">Back to top of page</span></a></p><br>

<h3 id="section1.2">2. What are the key features about Opening the Future that are of interest to librarians?</h3>

<p>The model is designed to service different types of libraries.</p>

<p>If you already have most or all of our backlist books it is likely that you would continue to buy our books in the future, but the more we can make OA the fewer books you would need to purchase. On the other hand, if you want a smaller selection of titles for a very economic price, then buying our backlist packages is for you. All the proceeds go to funding the OA frontlist and again this will broaden the range of books for your readers.</p>

<p>You can either purchase the backlist package(s) if your procurement policies require content in exchange for payment, or you can pay through your OA funds. And we offer an 'OA membership' option which comes with no backlist titles but which still helps to fund our open access efforts on the frontlist.</p>

<p>Either way, we are seeing a shift to supporting OA models from acquisitions budgets and we are hoping that this will be the source of support for OA, not only for us, but for all OA initiatives for books. Otherwise it will be impossible to move from pilots to regular practice. Regular revenue like this will greatly ease the way for new initiatives to become established and reliable funding for publishing activities.</p>

<p><a href="#top"><span style="font-size: 12px;">Back to top of page</span></a></p><br>

<h3 id="section1.3">3. How does Opening the Future help libraries use their budgets efficiently?</h3>

<p>Whether you're interested in acquiring additional backlist titles, or if you have them all already and want to support the frontlist going OA, the cost of our program is very attractive. Our members are banded according to the Carnegie Classification, as recognised by Lyrasis, and a three-year subscription for even the highest band library is equivalent to less than an industry average single BPC.</p>

<p><a href="#top"><span style="font-size: 12px;">Back to top of page</span></a></p><br>

<h3 id="section1.4">4. What is the goal of this model?</h3>

<p>Nothing less than to show a route to sustainable OA books for the foundational publications of the Humanities and Social Sciences.</p>

<p>We understand that as a small/medium-sized publisher we cannot change the world alone and that the next few years will require adjustments all along the supply chain including the role of intermediaries and aggregators. We are working closely with others to play an active part in this transition.</p>

<p><a href="#top"><span style="font-size: 12px;">Back to top of page</span></a></p><br>

<h3 id="section1.5">5. Is this like a 'Read and Publish' deal, but for books?</h3>

<p>No, the model is not based on the support of individual titles. If anything it's more like a 'Subscribe to Open' offer. Participating libraries get unlimited, DRM-free access to curated selections of backlist eBooks at a much cheaper price than buying them in print one at a time. The subscription fees are then used to publish new, frontlist books in OA format. It's that simple. There are no BPCs charged. Authors at participating libraries do not get 'preferential' or 'discounted' publishing deals: OA books are chosen on merit, through the normal editorial proposal process and are rigorously peer reviewed. The cost of producing OA books is paid for by the collected library subscription fees: so the more libraries sign up, the more books can be published as OA. If we can get to a fully open frontlist then any authors, including those based at subscribing member institutions, will be able to publish openly with us.</p>

<p><a href="#top"><span style="font-size: 12px;">Back to top of page</span></a></p><br>

<h3 id="section1.6">6. Can this model scale for use by other publishers without inundating libraries with lots of tiny deals?</h3>

<p>We believe that with the documented success of Opening the Future we have a model that could lead to the widespread transition of university and mission-driven presses worldwide to OA. Others, including the Copim Community and the Open Book Collective, are working on reducing the friction of OA publishing through managing and selling funding schemes to libraries.</p>

<p>Any publishers interested in launching an OtF program of their own can contact Fulcrum publishing services at <a href="https://www.fulcrum.org" target="_blank">fulcrum.org</a>.</p>

<h2>Opening the Future information for librarians</h2>

<h3 id="section2.1">1. When will you make books open access?</h3>

<p>All new frontlist titles are at first planned as traditionally-sold 'closed' books. But as soon as we have accrued enough library support to fund a book, after we identify a good candidate, we change the metadata before any sales are made and our distributors move the book to an OA status. This happens well before the book is published: so far, we've estimated between 2-3 months. In this way we hope to avoid libraries buying books because their OA status was announced too late.</p>

<p><a class="btn btn-primary" href="/oa-progress">Our forthcoming OA books</a></p>

<h3 id="section2.2">2. Is this program open to library consortia deals?</h3>

<p>Yes. We are in discussion with some library consortia already and are pleased to hear from any others. We aim to pass on any savings in administration costs to libraries. Please contact our Group Sales and Marketing Director to discuss: <a href="mailto:aking@boydellandbrewer.com">aking@boydellandbrewer.com</a></p>

<h3 id="section2.3">3. What happens if a frontlist title has access to other funding such as a BPC from a research grant?</h3>

<p>Our policy is to first seek funding from other sources and only if that is not available (which it is still not in most cases) would we apply the funds raised from this project to make books open.</p>

<p><a href="#top"><span style="font-size: 12px;">Back to top of page</span></a></p><br>

<h3 id="section2.4">4. How are your OA books chosen for publishing through OtF? What factors determine if a book should go open?</h3>

<p>Our goal is to make high-quality scholarly research freely accessible to the global academic community. Titles selected for Open Access (OA) under Opening the Future are chosen based on clear principles that reflect both scholarly value and community priorities.</p>

<h4>Selection Principles</h4>

<ol>
<li><strong>Equity and Accessibility</strong><br>
We prioritize works that amplify underrepresented voices, explore marginalized communities, or address regions and topics often overlooked in mainstream academic publishing.</li>
<li><strong>Academic and Disciplinary Impact</strong><br>
Books that align with current research priorities—such as sustainability, social justice, and interdisciplinary studies—are given preference, ensuring relevance for teaching and research across multiple disciplines.</li>
<li><strong>Author and Institutional Diversity</strong><br>
We aim to focus on those authors with limited access to OA funding, whether they be early-career/first time authors or established scholars.</li>
</ol>

<h4>Process</h4>

<ol>
<li>We assess which titles would most benefit from being openly accessible as per the above principles, in a given timeline (when funding has reached a threshold that a title can be considered).</li>
<li>We consult with authors to confirm their interest in OA, exclude titles with existing OA funding support, and check there are no copyright obstacles to Open Access provision.</li>
<li>All OA titles funded through Opening the Future undergo the same rigorous editorial and peer review processes as our traditionally published books.</li>
</ol>

<h4>Library Impact</h4>

<p>Your support directly enables these high-impact titles to become freely available worldwide, extending their reach far beyond traditional purchase and subscription models and advancing equitable access to scholarship.</p>

<p><a href="#top"><span style="font-size: 12px;">Back to top of page</span></a></p><br>

<h3 id="section2.5">5. How can the Press revenue targets be reached? And what happens if and when they are reached? If it is not reached, how does the Press decide what will be published OA?</h3>

<p>Revenue targets can be reached as more libraries join up. The model is incremental by design and this is one of its USPs, with a low flipping threshold that makes it quick for members to see positive results of their investment.</p>

<p>If subscription revenue exceeds the costs spent on publishing OA frontlist in any given year we will discuss with our members on how best to spend that, for example by publishing more books OA; reducing fees for members; or re-investing the surplus money into open infrastructures.</p>

<p>We have several relevant criteria regarding what titles to make available OA through OtF funding; primarily that the authors want (or need) to publish their monographs OA, and that there is no other funding available. After that, we assign titles in order of publication date, with as much notice to the libraries as possible through our metadata distributions to ensure they are aware of the status change to OA.</p>

<h2>Signing up - money and conditions</h2>

<h3 id="section3.1">1. What do subscribing members get for their money?</h3>

<p>Members receive access to between 30-150 titles (depending on the number of packages subscribed to) from Boydell &amp; Brewer's backlist on medieval studies, performance and music, and African politics and economics.</p>

<p><a href="#top"><span style="font-size: 12px;">Back to top of page</span></a></p><br>

<p>Library and institutional members are banded according to Carnegie Classification, as recognised by Lyrasis. Based on this, our membership fees for 2026 are:</p>

<ul>
<li>$1350 high tier, per year (£935 GBP)</li>

They will be entitled to perpetual access to that package at the end of their three year membership. You may sign up for access to a separate package at any time - this membership and package access will also be for a minimum of three years.</p>

<h3 id="section3.3">3. Will member libraries/institutions have unlimited access to the books in the subscription packages? And what happens at the end of three years?</h3>

<p>Member libraries and institutions have unlimited concurrent/simultaneous access to all titles in the package(s) you've subscribed to during the term of your three-year membership. You will be entitled to perpetual access to the package(s) at the end of your three year membership. There is no 'bait and switch' and packages won't suddenly change after you have joined.</p>

<h3 id="section3.4">4. What will be the format of the book subscription package and what services will be available to members?</h3>

<p>The books in the subscription packages are hosted on Fulcrum in their standard DRM-free, unlimited-use model for ebooks. Content is delivered in chapter-based PDF and EPUB formats. Full-text searching is available within individual titles and across title-level metadata. Fulcrum supports authentication via IP, Shibboleth, OpenAthens ID, and referring URL. Participating libraries will be able to access MARC records and KBART files customised to their holdings, and to retrieve COUNTER 5-compliant usage statistics.</p>

<p>Fulcrum collaborates with major library discovery vendors and will ensure the packages are set up as collections to be activated in all pertinent discovery services. Books on the Fulcrum platform are preserved through CLOCKSS.</p>

<p>Fulcrum is committed to the accessibility of content and aims to meet the latest WCAG 2AA Standards (currently WCAG 2.2 AA). They have a full accessibility statement on their website:</p>

<p><a class="btn btn-primary" href="https://www.fulcrum.org/accessibility/" target="_blank">Go to Fulcrum site</a></p>

<p><a href="#top"><span style="font-size: 12px;">Back to top of page</span></a></p><br>

<p>We have multiple packages available, and libraries are free to subscribe to multiple. We also have some combined backlist packages if you would like to subscribe to several packages at once, and an OA supporter package without backlist.</p>

<h2>About the books and collections</h2>

<h3 id="section4.1">1. How have the packages been composed?</h3>

<p>Having determined which subjects we wanted to focus our packages on, we put the packages together by focussing on backlist that formed a coherent subject package, and which were still useful and relevant scholarship. We also opted to include only titles with EPUB formats to ensure that our packages were highly accessible.</p>

<p><a href="#top"><span style="font-size: 12px;">Back to top of page</span></a></p><br>

<h3 id="section4.2">2. Are there any DRM restrictions on the backlist?</h3>

<p>No, all backlist package titles that you are subscribed to will be DRM-free and accessible by multiple users simultaneously.</p>

<p><a href="#top"><span style="font-size: 12px;">Back to top of page</span></a></p><br>

<h3 id="section4.3">3. How will the OA books be made available? And how are supporting libraries informed?</h3>

<p>The new titles funded by the program to be published open access will be available in PDF format with CC BY NC ND licences. Fulcrum supports open access books with MARC records, KBART files, and metadata sharing with major library vendors, to ensure that OA content is widely discoverable through library systems.</p>

<p>We update our progress page on the website, and issue news items as well as send details through listservs. We also include a note of new OA titles in our librarian newsletter, which you should receive as a confirmed customer. All titles get distributed to as wide a network as possible, this includes JSTOR, OAPEN, DOAB, EBSCO and ProQuest - this way, if you are set up to receive catalogue data from any of these services, you will get details of our OA titles as well wherever you choose to make them available.</p>

<p>You can keep track of our OA publishing progress at:</p>

<p><a class="btn btn-primary" href="/oa-progress">Our forthcoming OA books</a></p>

<h2>About Boydell &amp; Brewer and OtF partners</h2>

<h3 id="section6.1">1. Who are the other partners?</h3>

<p>This collaboration on the OtF program aligns with Boydell &amp; Brewer’s commitment to expanding open access publishing in a sustainable way, ensuring that more high-quality content can be made accessible globally. The backlist books are hosted on Michigan Publishing’s community-based, open-source Fulcrum platform - seamlessly delivering accessible content into library catalogues. The program is supported by the trusted non-profit library consortium Lyrasis based in the USA, and membership is open to libraries worldwide. OA books are made available on OAPEN, DOAB, JSTOR and other platforms.</p>

<p><a href="#top"><span style="font-size: 12px;">Back to top of page</span></a></p><br>

<h3 id="section6.2">2. Why do we get invoicing help from partners like Lyrasis?</h3>

<p>Transitioning to open access is hard and we're in the midst of a transition where we need to accommodate the needs of libraries. We understand that libraries often need to go through extensive deliberations amongst their own stakeholders to see if it is worth investing in our particular offer. We don't have the resources to talk to every librarian, so we rely on trusted library partners like Lyrasis who are already communicating with librarians every day.</p>

<p><a href="#top"><span style="font-size: 12px;">Back to top of page</span></a></p><br>

<h3 id="section6.3">3. Who is supporting Opening the Future at Boydell &amp; Brewer?</h3>

<p>The backlist books are hosted on Michigan Publishing’s community-based, open-source Fulcrum platform - seamlessly delivering accessible content into library catalogues. The program is supported by the trusted non-profit library consortium Lyrasis based in the USA, and membership is open to libraries worldwide.</p>

<p>Michigan Publishing is the Publishing division of the University of Michigan Library. We support the broadest possible access to trustworthy knowledge by promoting deep engagement with the expertise of authors and creators, a positive environment in which human dignity is valued, and constant improvement in the structure and clarity of publishing processes. With our Fulcrum technology and services platform, Michigan Publishing provides mission-driven publishers like Boydell &amp; Brewer with a hosting solution driven by five core design principles: flexibility, durability, discoverability, accessibility, and integrity.</p>

<p>Lyrasis is a community-supported membership organization whose mission is to support enduring access to the world’s shared academic, scientific and cultural heritage through leadership in open technologies, content services, digital solutions and collaboration with archives, libraries, museums and knowledge communities worldwide.</p>

<p>The OtF model was originally devised and launched by the Copim team in 2021 and was shortlisted for an ALPSP Innovation in Publishing Award soon afterwards. Boydell &amp; Brewer are the first non-University Press to take up the model, opening the pathway for other mission-driven, scholarly publishers. When we first launched this program as a pilot with one press in 2021 we always hoped that other scholarly publishers would take it up and use OtF to fund more OA books. This partnership between Fulcrum, Lyrasis and Boydell &amp; Brewer is an important step in sustaining and normalizing the model beyond the original project start-up phase.</p>

<p>You can find out more about the Copim Community and its other open infrastructures at <a href="https://copim.ac.uk" target="_blank">copim.ac.uk</a>.</p>
